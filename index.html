Key Takeaways


Literary devices like verbal irony, metaphor, simile, allusion, and pun are the magical tools that writers use to make their stories and poems exciting, creative, and fun to read. They help us imagine things better, feel emotions deeply, and sometimes even laugh out loud!
Let’s quickly review these with examples:
Verbal Irony: Saying one thing but meaning the opposite.
  • Example: When it’s pouring rain, you say, “What a lovely day!”
Metaphor: Comparing two things directly without using "like" or "as."
  • Example: "Her smile was sunshine," means her smile was bright and warm.
Simile: Comparing two things using "like" or "as."
  • Example: "The kitten was as fluffy as a cloud."
Allusion: Referring to something well-known from history, literature, or culture.
  • Example: "He was a true King Arthur in bravery," refers to the famous, heroic king.
Pun: A joke that plays with the meanings or sounds of words.
  • Example: "I’m reading a book about anti-gravity. It’s impossible to put down!"

Words that I Learnt from the Story 📚

Muttered
Meaning: To speak quietly and unclearly, often when complaining or talking to yourself.
Example: She muttered under her breath when she didn’t get her turn in the game.

Gleaming
Meaning: Shining brightly, often because it is clean or polished.
Example: The freshly cleaned car was gleaming under the sunlight.

Stomped
Meaning: To walk with heavy, loud steps, often because you are angry or upset.
Example: He stomped into the room after losing the race, making the floor shake.
